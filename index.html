Shannon QA · runs in your browser · nothing is sent to a server

Ask a document a question. Get an answer from the text, or an honest “not answerable”.

Paste any text and ask about it. Shannon QA finds the passages that matter, lets a small reading model propose answers, and a learned gate picks one: a span, a list, a number, yes or no, or none. There is no generative model, so every answer is a piece of your text, and when no candidate is good enough it says so.

your text→passages→overlap + meaning retrieval→reader proposes answers→yes/no model→gate: pick one, or refuse
Three small models, downloaded once and kept in your browser's cache.

    How it answers

    1 · passages

    Split by length only

    The text's own paragraphs, long ones cut into windows of whole sentences (up to 120 words), short lines such as headings carried into the next passage. No rules about content.

    2 · retrieval

    Overlap and meaning

    Top 3 passages by word overlap, weighted by how rare each word is in this document, plus top 3 by MiniLM sentence-embedding similarity.

    all-MiniLM-L6-v2 · 22.7M parameters

    3 · candidates

    The reader proposes

    An extractive QA model reads each passage and proposes up to 6 spans. Each becomes a span, a one-item list, a number (via a small number-word reader), and pairs become two-item lists.

    minilm-uncased-squad2 · 33.2M

    4 · yes / no

    A cross-encoder judges

    A DeBERTa-v3 model fine-tuned on public BoolQ plus development yes/no questions gives P(yes) for the top passages.

    DeBERTa-v3-xsmall · 70.8M

    5 · choice

    The gate picks or refuses

    A question-form model (from the question's embedding) and a 50-weight log-linear gate score every candidate, including none. The top score wins; its lead over the runner-up is the confidence.

    This is a port of the Python system (treaty-graph's no-hand-code system) to JavaScript, run with onnxruntime-web. On 66 test questions over the three example texts it gives the same answer as the Python system every time (see the notes below).

    04

    Honest notes

    No generative model. Shannon QA can only point at words in your text. It cannot explain, summarise, combine facts from far apart, or answer “why” questions well. When nothing fits, it refuses.
    Built on treaties and legal texts. Its gate and yes/no model were trained on questions about treaties, the US Constitution, an OAuth RFC and the laws of chess. It is weaker on other kinds of writing: try the Moon text, where it misses several easy questions.
    The first two examples flatter it. The UN Charter and the US Constitution were development documents: the gate saw other questions about them. The Moon text was written for this page and never used in development.

    Published results (from the project's pre-registered exam write-ups)

    un_4: two treaties, 348 questionsaccuracy
    Shannon QA (the same design, trained without un_4)73.0%
    Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, given the same passages (reference)60.9%
    Baseline: minilm-squad2 with overlap retrieval51.1%
    • un_4 was used as development data before this design was fixed, so 73.0 is a development number, not a held-out exam result. The model on this page was also trained on un_4 and on other development sets. The 60.9 and 51.1 rows come from the pre-registered un_4 scoring.
    • Much of the lead comes from refusing: Shannon QA refused 84.8% of the un_4 questions the text could not answer.
    • Reliability (lower AURC is better: fewer wrong answers among the most confident ones): the earlier treaty-specific system, H4, scored AURC 0.199 against 0.310 for Qwen2.5-1.5B on un_4. That is not this system. For this system, a later check against a calibrated, 4-shot Qwen2.5-1.5B found the two level (cross-fit AURC 0.180 vs 0.172), so no reliability win is claimed.
    • Results on new domains (two sealed exams) are not reported here.

    This page

    • Models: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (Apache-2.0), deepset/minilm-uncased-squad2 (CC-BY-4.0, by deepset), and a yes/no cross-encoder fine-tuned from cross-encoder/nli-deberta-v3-xsmall (Apache-2.0) on BoolQ (CC-BY-SA-3.0). They are stored as fp16 weights, plus int8 for the yes/no model's word table, and computed in fp32. Plain int8 throughout moved the reader's spans and the yes/no probabilities too much, so it isn't used.
    • The confidence shown is the gate's margin: how far the winning candidate's score is above the runner-up's. It is not a probability.
    • Everything runs single-threaded in WebAssembly. A question takes about one second on a laptop and longer on a phone. Reading a long document takes a few seconds.

    Shannon QA is a research system. Example texts: the Charter of the United Nations (Articles 1–32); the Constitution of the United States (National Archives transcription, public domain); “The Moon”, written for this page. Source on GitHub · Shannon's idea model.
